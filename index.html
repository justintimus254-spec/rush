Two stars · one chef de partie

Rush

You run every station in a two-star kitchen. Tickets print at the pass, and every steak, noodle, scallop and soufflé cooks on a real clock. Cook each plate to the ticket before the table walks out.

Ribeye, medium rare5:00 – 6:00 Spaghetti, al dente8:00 – 10:00 Foie gras, seared1:10 – 1:50 Chocolate soufflé, risen12:00 – 14:00 Beef Wellington, medium rare25:00 – 27:00
  1. Grab raw food from the pantry on your left: meat and seafood in the walk-in, pasta, produce and pastry in the dry store.
  2. Cook it on the right station along the line in front of you: grill, griddle, range (pans and pots from the rack behind you), fryer, noodles and wok, or the oven on your right. Raw-bar items get prepped on the cold station on your right.
  3. Flip steaks, chops and seared fish once, about halfway through. Tap the item on the grill, griddle or pan to flip it.
  4. Rest meat on the resting board for 2:00 before plating it.
  5. Set a clean plate on the ticket's spot at the pass behind you, plate everything on it, then finish it with the right sauce and garnish from the rail behind the plates. Keep the sauces stirred or they break.

Chef Ashford runs the pass and calls every order out loud. Answer “Yes, Chef!” every time: press Y, tap the button, or press A or X in VR. Ignore him or wreck plates and he gets angrier. If his patience runs out, he throws you off the line. Line cooks can help: tap Marco (sous chef, careful) or Theo (commis, fast but sloppy), and Ana once you hire her, and they'll cook the next item on the most urgent ticket. You still sauce and garnish.

Want a break from the line? Tap Bar shift to work the bar: free-pour spirits by tapping a bottle to start and again to stop, then build, shake or stir, garnish and serve.

During the rush you'll get big parties, send-backs, allergy tickets, grease fires, food running out, and a famous food critic. Every plate is graded and every event is logged in your end-of-shift report.

Kitchen time runs 10× faster than real time. On a computer, click to grab and place. Drag, press A/D or the arrow keys, or use the number keys to look around, and press P to pause. Press C on the menu to play the hands-off showcase reel for recording videos.

In a headset: tap Enter VR. Reach out and squeeze the grip (or pinch with your bare hand) to grab things, and let go over a station or plate to set them down, or toss them. The trigger still points and taps from a distance. Press A or X to answer Chef, and B or Y to pause.

Mind the kitchen: drop food on the floor and it's ruined. The cooks call “Behind!” and step around you, but walk into someone and you can fumble what you're carrying, and a careless swing of the hand knocks food and pans flying. Hot metal burns bare hands, so grab the side towel from the hook on the griddle for hot pans. Burnt food smokes, and fires spread to the next station, the towel and grease on the floor: grab the extinguisher by the bin and keep it in hand until every fire is out. Grease makes people slip, so mop it up.

Around the line: the big griddle in the middle cooks bacon, eggs, pancakes, cheesesteak, smash patties, crab cakes and scallops straight on the steel. Chop cold-station food with the chef's knife, slap the bell on the pass to hurry the runners, and string perfect plates together for a heat streak (and a high five from Chef).

Career: start in the dish pit and work your way up: dishwasher, prep cook, commis, line cook, sous chef, head chef. Two shifts graded B or better at each rank earn a promotion. Before every service, Chef gathers the brigade for a briefing: tonight's menu, the special, bookings and allergies, and which station you're on.

Play together: up to three chefs can share the kitchen. One taps Play together → Host a kitchen and reads out the 4-digit room code; friends tap Join with a code. Each friend who joins replaces one of the computer cooks.
